Leading a beginner enduro ride

Chiang Mai Enduro Riders · rules for group leaders

Rules for group leaders, in the order you'll need them.

🧠 Mindset📱 Before⛽ Meeting point🚦 Trail start🏍️ On the trail🍻 End
🧠

Mindset

1
Lead for them, not for you

Leading a group is community service. Your goal shifts from “I want to have fun riding” to “I want everyone else to have fun riding.” You won't ride as fast or as freely as you'd like; the reward is the teamwork and getting past obstacles together.

2
Never lose a rider

Your goal is to complete the ride without losing any rider. As a leader you have to ALWAYS wait for the whole group on every intersection (unless you are turning right, see the “always turn right” rule below) and make sure everyone sees which way you went.

3
If you lose a rider

Ask the group to take a break where they are and go back looking for the lost rider, following the GPS track in reverse.

📱

Before the ride

4
Master offline navigation
  • If you can't reliably follow the GPS track you can't lead.
  • Fit a sturdy phone holder to your handlebar and a USB charger to your bike, or your phone's battery may die mid-ride.
  • Install a GPS navigation app that works offline and supports OpenStreetMap (e.g., Gaia GPS on iPhone, Locus Map on Android).
  • A few days before the ride, create a short test track around your neighborhood, put your phone in flight mode to simulate no signal, and ride it until you can follow it reliably.
  • The day before the ride, load the ride's GPS track and check that it shows up and navigation starts.
  • If you have a spare phone take it with you and have the same GPS app and track already installed there.
⛽

At the meeting point

5
Brief the group on ride difficulty

Tell the whole group how long the ride will take and how difficult it will be. Mention all the expected difficulties like terrain, uphills or obstacles. Everyone should know what they are signing up to.

6
Check the whole group

Check that every rider has:

  • 100% full tank of gas
  • food and at least 3 liters of water
  • the recommended bike: a CRF or lighter with 50/50 or 100% enduro tyres (heavier bikes like 701 are allowed only for riders with a lot of off-road experience)
  • the minimum gear: high boots, knee protection, helmet and gloves

Anyone who doesn't meet these requirements can't join. Send them home because they put the whole ride at risk.

7
Find the sweeping rider

Identify the more experienced riders and ask one of them to be the sweeping rider. They will be riding last for the rest of the ride. They might be grumpy because it's less fun, but sweeping is a position of responsibility, second only to the leader, as they often find themselves helping struggling riders and the rest of the group when the leader is not present. Try to identify the most helpful and responsible person, he will be your second in command.

If a pair of radios were provided by the organizers, keep one radio for you and give the other to the sweeping rider, asking them to let you know every time the rear of the group stops, so that you can also stop and wait for them to catch up with the head.

🚦

At the start of the trail

8
Explain the riding rules to the group

Before you set off on the trail, explain these two rules to the whole group.

A. “Always turn right” rule

The rule: at a junction, no leader waiting means the group took the right-most trail. So, at every junction:

  • If the route takes the right-most trail, turn without stopping. By right-most we mean the trail closest to your right hand (which might be also in front of you in case of a Y intersection)
  • If it takes any other trail, stop and wait for the whole group. When you see the last rider coming, set off on the correct trail and the group follows.
B. “Help front rider” rule
  • Group rule: if the rider in front of you drops their bike in a difficult spot, always stop, get off and help lift it. It saves everyone's energy. Each rider should remember to help their buddy in front.
  • As leader, stop after every significant obstacle, such as a hard climb, and wait until everyone is through. If someone is struggling, help.
9
Before starting the ride activate the GPS recording.

When GPS recording most apps will be displaying a line on the screen showing where you rode up to this point. This line is very useful to orient yourself on the map to see where you came from should you need to backtrack.

Also, your GPS recording helps us to keep the track updated. If you find the track blocked we would see which detour you took to avoid the obstacle.

🏍️

On the trail

10
Take a break every 1–2 hours
  • Stop for 15 minutes every 1–2 hours, or right after an obstacle that wore the group out.
  • Announce how long the break is. When time's up, rally the group, politely interrupting conversations if needed; otherwise the break drags on.
  • Use the break to check how everyone is doing and whether anyone has a problem.
11
If you took a wrong turn or lose the track

It's always better to go back and find the track, instead of continuing the wrong way hoping to catch the track further ahead, because most of the time you will not find it, or end up skipping a big section of the ride.

Don't worry about making mistakes and turning the whole group around and backtrack, everyone knows that off-road GPS navigation is difficult and they don't mind.

12
Stick to the plan

Riders may suggest changes to the route, trying to “hijack” the ride. Push back politely because everyone signed up for this specific route on this day, and it's your job keeping this commitment for the group.

🍻

At the end

13
Finish together

Plan a brief drinks stop at one of the Thai shops on the way home so the group can socialize before heading off.
